Who Was Involved

The People Behind the Wall

The Berlin Wall was not created by one person alone. Its history involved political leaders, government officials, soldiers, ordinary citizens, activists, and people who tried to cross the border. The decisions made by political leaders shaped the history of Berlin, while the actions of ordinary people showed the human consequences of a divided city.

Walter Ulbricht

Walter Ulbricht was one of the most important political leaders in East Germany during the construction of the Berlin Wall. He served as the country's leader from 1950 until 1971. Ulbricht was a strong supporter of the communist system and maintained close political ties with the Soviet Union. His government was increasingly concerned about the large number of people leaving East Germany through Berlin. In the summer of 1961, the East German leadership decided to close the border between East and West Berlin. Ulbricht played a central role in the political decisions that led to the construction of the wall. The new border was presented by the East German government as a measure designed to protect the country and prevent Western influence. For many people in East Germany, however, it became a symbol of restricted freedom and separation.

He famously replied:

“Nobody intends to put up a wall!”

Nikita Khrushchev

Nikita Khrushchev was the leader of the Soviet Union during the period when the Berlin Wall was built. The Soviet Union was the most powerful country in the communist Eastern Bloc, and East Germany depended heavily on Soviet political and military support. Berlin was one of the major points of tension between the Soviet Union and the Western powers. Khrushchev wanted to strengthen the position of East Germany and reduce the ability of people to leave the communist country through West Berlin. The Soviet leadership supported the East German government's decision to close the border. Although the wall was built by East German authorities, the Soviet Union's influence was an important part of the political background behind the decision.

John F. Kennedy

Listen to History

In 1963, U.S. President John F. Kennedy visited West Berlin and delivered his famous speech. In this recording, he says:

“Ich bin ein Berliner.”

Why Was Kennedy's Speech Important?

Kennedy's speech showed American support for the people of West Berlin and became one of the most famous speeches of the Cold War.

John F. Kennedy was the President of the United States when the Berlin Wall was constructed in 1961. The United States regarded West Berlin as an important symbol of its commitment to its European allies. Kennedy strongly opposed the spread of Soviet influence and supported the continued presence of American forces in West Berlin. When the wall was built, Kennedy did not attempt to remove it by military force. Instead, the United States maintained its position in West Berlin and emphasized that the Western powers would continue to defend the city's freedom. In 1963, Kennedy visited West Berlin and delivered a famous speech expressing solidarity with the people of the divided city. His visit became an important moment in the history of Berlin.
