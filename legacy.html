What Still Remains

A Wall That No Longer Divides

The Berlin Wall stood for almost 28 years, but today it no longer divides the city. Most of the original wall was removed after its opening in 1989, and Berlin has changed dramatically since then. However, the history of the wall has not disappeared. Parts of it have been preserved throughout Berlin, while museums, photographs, documents, and personal stories continue to tell the story of the divided city. What remains today is not only concrete. The Berlin Wall has become a reminder of a period when one city, one country, and much of Europe were divided by political differences.

The East Side Gallery

visit the nuesum

One of the most famous remaining sections of the Berlin Wall is the East Side Gallery. It is a long preserved section of the former wall covered with colorful murals created by artists from different countries. The artworks were painted after the wall opened and express ideas about freedom, hope, peace, and political change. The East Side Gallery has become an important cultural and historical landmark in Berlin. Visitors can walk alongside the wall, see the artwork, and remember what once stood in its place. The colorful paintings also show how the meaning of the wall changed. A structure that once represented division and restriction became a place where people could express themselves freely.

"Anyone wishing to gain a deeper understanding of the history of the Berlin Wall and the culture that developed around it will not want to miss a visit to the museum."

Memorials and Museums

Several museums and memorial sites in Berlin preserve the history of the Berlin Wall. These places contain photographs, documents, personal objects, and pieces of the original border system. Some also preserve sections of the wall and parts of the former border area. The exhibitions help visitors understand what daily life was like in a divided Berlin. They also tell the stories of people who lived on either side of the border and those who attempted to cross it. These museums are important because many people who visit Berlin today were not alive when the wall existed. The physical objects and personal stories allow new generations to learn about a period that might otherwise feel very distant.

Traces Across Berlin

Even though most of the wall has disappeared, traces of it can still be found throughout Berlin. A line of bricks and metal markers in the streets often shows where the wall once stood. Some former watchtowers, sections of the wall, and other parts of the border system have also been preserved. Walking through modern Berlin, visitors can sometimes cross the former border without even realizing it. These small traces are important because they connect the modern city with its divided past. They show that history does not always survive as a large monument. Sometimes, it can be found in a simple line across a street.
